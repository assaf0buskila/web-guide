# Writing Guide — Creating Five-Layer Optimized Content

Use this guide when helping the user create new web content from scratch. Follow these phases in order — each one builds on the previous.

---

## Phase 1: Strategic Foundation

Before writing a single word, establish these elements:

### 1.1 Define the Content's Purpose
Ask and answer:
- **Primary intent**: Informational, navigational, commercial investigation, or transactional?
- **Target audience**: Who is this for? What do they already know? What do they need?
- **Success metric**: What does "working" look like? Traffic? Citations? Conversions? Brand authority?
- **Competitive landscape**: What already exists on this topic? What can we add that's different?

### 1.2 Query Universe Mapping
Identify the full set of queries this content should serve:

- **Primary query**: The main keyword/question this page targets
- **Secondary queries**: Related questions that should be answered within the content
- **Long-tail variations**: Specific, conversational versions of the query (these are what voice and AI searches use)
- **Entity queries**: Searches about the key entities mentioned in the content

**Pattern for discovering queries:**
Think about the topic from the user's perspective at different stages:
- "What is [topic]?" (awareness)
- "How does [topic] work?" (understanding)
- "[Topic] vs [alternative]" (comparison)
- "Best [topic] for [use case]" (evaluation)
- "How to [action] with [topic]" (implementation)

### 1.3 Content Architecture Planning
Before writing, plan the structure:

- **Title (H1)**: Should include primary query, match intent, and be compelling. Under 60 characters.
- **Section outline (H2s)**: Each H2 should address one major subtopic or question. Aim for 4-8 H2 sections.
- **Sub-sections (H3s)**: Break complex H2 sections into scannable sub-sections.
- **Answer plan**: For each section, note the key claim or answer in one sentence. This becomes your extractable core.

## Phase 2: Writing for All Five Layers

### 2.1 Opening Section (Critical for GEO + AEO)

The first 100-150 words of your content carry disproportionate weight. They must:

1. **Open with a definition-lead sentence** that answers the primary query directly.
   - Pattern: "[Topic] is a [category] that [differentiator]."
   - Example: "Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the practice of structuring content so that AI search systems select and cite it when generating answers to user queries."

2. **Expand with 2-3 sentences** that add context, scope, or urgency.

3. **Include a specificity anchor** — a concrete data point, date, or verifiable fact in the first paragraph.
   - Weak: "AI search is growing rapidly."
   - Strong: "AI-referred sessions to websites grew 527% year-over-year through mid-2025, and major AI platforms now process billions of queries monthly."

4. **Signal what the content delivers** — tell the reader (and the AI) what they'll get from this page.

**Example opening:**

> Content velocity is the practice of increasing both the speed and volume of content production without sacrificing quality or strategic alignment. In 2026, content teams that publish 4+ optimized pieces per week see 3.2x more AI citations than those publishing weekly or less. This guide covers the frameworks, workflows, and tooling that high-performing content teams use to scale output — including the specific structural patterns that maximize both search rankings and AI citation rates.

This opening is extractable (an AI can cite any sentence), definitional (it answers "what is content velocity?"), data-anchored (specific multiplier), and intent-signaling (tells the reader exactly what follows).

### 2.2 Body Sections (All Five Layers)

For each major section:

#### Start with the answer (AEO)
Lead every section with the core answer or claim in the first 2-3 sentences. Don't build up to it — state it, then support it. This is the "inverted pyramid" adapted for AI extraction.

**Pattern:**
```
## [Question or Topic as H2]
[Direct answer in 1-2 sentences — extractable as a standalone snippet]
[Supporting evidence, context, or explanation in 2-4 sentences]
[Deeper detail, examples, or nuance in remaining paragraphs]
```

**Example:**

> ## How Long Does AEO Take to Show Results?
> Most AEO optimizations begin showing measurable changes in AI citation rates within 6-8 weeks, though Featured Snippet improvements can appear in as little as 2-3 weeks. The timeline depends on the competitiveness of the query, the authority of your domain, and whether the content already ranks in traditional search.
>
> Content that already ranks in the top 10 for a target query typically gets picked up by AI engines faster because retrieval systems often prioritize content that has passed Google's quality filters. Net-new content on domains with lower authority may take 3-4 months to build sufficient signals.

Notice: the first sentence alone could be extracted as a complete answer. The rest adds depth for users who want it.

#### Include statistical anchors (GEO)
Every major section should contain at least one specific, verifiable data point. This dramatically increases citability.

- Name the source: "According to [Source]..." or "[Study/Organization] found that..."
- Use precise numbers: percentages, timeframes, sample sizes
- Date the data: "In Q1 2026..." or "A 2025 study by..."

#### Make comparisons structured (AIO)
When comparing options, features, or approaches — use tables, not prose.

**Weak (prose comparison):**
> SEO focuses on rankings while AEO focuses on answers and GEO focuses on citations. They differ in their metrics too, with SEO tracking position and traffic, AEO tracking snippet capture rate, and GEO tracking citation frequency.

**Strong (table comparison):**

| Aspect | SEO | AEO | GEO |
|--------|-----|-----|-----|
| Goal | Rank in SERPs | Be the direct answer | Get cited by AI |
| Primary metric | Position + traffic | Snippet capture rate | Citation frequency |
| Content format | Comprehensive pages | Concise Q&A pairs | Data-backed, citable claims |
| Time to impact | 3-6 months | 2-8 weeks | 6-12 weeks |

Tables are parseable, scannable, and extractable — they serve humans and machines equally well.

#### Maintain entity consistency (AIO)
When you introduce an entity, define it once and use that name consistently:

- First mention: "Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of..."
- Subsequent mentions: "AEO" — not "it," "the practice," "this approach," or "answer optimization"
- If you must use a pronoun, make sure the antecedent is unambiguous within the same paragraph

#### Add unique value (GEO — Information Gain)
In every section, ask: "What does this page say that 50 other pages don't?" If the answer is "nothing," add:
- An original framework or mental model
- A first-person case study or example
- A contrarian or nuanced perspective backed by evidence
- A practical tool, template, or checklist
- An expert quote or interview excerpt

### 2.3 FAQ Section (AEO + GEO Power Move)

Every piece of content should end with (or contain) a FAQ section of 5-8 questions. These are Featured Snippet magnets and AI extraction goldmines.

**Rules for effective FAQ answers:**
1. Each answer must be completely self-contained — no "as mentioned above," no "yes, it does" without restating what "it" is
2. Answers should be 2-5 sentences, stating the answer in sentence one and supporting it in the rest
3. Include at least one specific fact or data point per answer
4. Write in natural, conversational language (voice search ready)
5. Cover questions from different intent angles (what, how, why, when, who, comparison)

**Example of a well-optimized FAQ entry:**

> **How is AEO different from SEO?**
> AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) focuses on making content appear as direct answers in Featured Snippets, voice assistant responses, and AI-generated summaries, while SEO (Search Engine Optimization) focuses on ranking web pages higher in search result listings. The key difference is the end goal: SEO aims for clicks, AEO aims for citations and zero-click visibility. Both disciplines share foundational practices like quality content and structured data, but AEO requires additional optimization for extractability — ensuring each answer can stand alone when pulled out of the page context.

This answer works because: it defines both terms, draws a clear contrast, acknowledges overlap, and introduces "extractability" as a unique concept — all in a self-contained paragraph.

## Phase 3: SXO — Experience Layer

After the content is written, evaluate and optimize the user experience:

### 3.1 Scannability Pass
Review the content and ensure:
- No paragraph exceeds 4-5 sentences
- Every 2-3 paragraphs are broken by a heading, image, table, or visual break
- Bold is used sparingly for key terms or takeaways (not entire sentences)
- The page can be "read" in 30 seconds by scanning headings and bold terms alone

### 3.2 Conversion Architecture
Based on the content's intent, design the conversion path:

- **Informational content**: End sections with natural bridges to related content ("Learn how to implement this in our [guide to X]"). Include a newsletter CTA or content upgrade (checklist, template) at the bottom.
- **Commercial investigation content**: Include comparison tables, "best for" positioning, and a clear CTA to try/buy. Place CTAs after value-demonstrating sections, not at the very top.
- **Transactional content**: Minimize distractions. Clear pricing/value, social proof, and frictionless action buttons. Remove anything that doesn't serve the conversion.

### 3.3 Trust Architecture
Embed trust signals throughout:
- Author byline with credentials (not just a name — include relevant expertise)
- Publication and last-updated dates (visible, not hidden)
- Source citations for factual claims
- Social proof where relevant (user counts, testimonials, logos)
- Transparent, honest tone (acknowledge limitations, don't oversell)

## Phase 4: Pre-Publication Checklist

Before the content goes live, verify each layer:

### SEO Checks
- [ ] Title tag includes primary keyword, under 60 characters, compelling
- [ ] Meta description summarizes value, under 155 characters, includes CTA
- [ ] H1 matches the primary search intent
- [ ] H2/H3 hierarchy is logical and includes secondary keywords naturally
- [ ] Primary keyword appears in first 100 words, in at least one H2, and naturally throughout
- [ ] Images have descriptive alt text and are compressed
- [ ] Internal links connect to 3+ related pages on the site
- [ ] URL is clean and keyword-relevant

### AEO Checks
- [ ] The primary question is answered directly in the first 2-3 sentences
- [ ] At least 3 sections use question-as-heading + direct answer format
- [ ] FAQ section has 5+ self-contained Q&A pairs
- [ ] Answers work when extracted in isolation (no context dependencies)
- [ ] Content reads naturally if spoken aloud (voice-ready)

### GEO Checks
- [ ] Opening paragraph contains a definition-lead sentence
- [ ] At least 5 specific, verifiable data points with named sources
- [ ] Content includes at least one element of unique value (original data, framework, case study)
- [ ] Key claims are stated in confident, citable language (no excessive hedging)
- [ ] Content has been updated or created within the last 90 days (recency signal)
- [ ] Brand/author entity is clearly identified

### SXO Checks
- [ ] Page loads in under 2 seconds on mobile
- [ ] First screen clearly communicates what the page is about
- [ ] Content is scannable (short paragraphs, clear headings, visual breaks)
- [ ] Clear CTA aligned with content intent
- [ ] No unnecessary friction (pop-ups, required registration, broken elements)
- [ ] Mobile experience is fully functional

### AIO Checks
- [ ] Content is organized in self-contained, independently parseable modules
- [ ] Comparisons use tables, not prose
- [ ] Entities are defined on first mention and named consistently throughout
- [ ] Dates are explicit (not "recently" or "this year")
- [ ] Data points are precise and unambiguous
- [ ] Structured data opportunities have been identified (FAQ, HowTo, Product, etc.)

## Content Patterns Library

### Pattern: The Definitive Answer Block
Use when the content's primary job is to answer a specific question.

```
[H2: The question, verbatim as people ask it]
[Sentence 1: Direct answer — the extractable snippet]
[Sentence 2-3: Key supporting evidence or context]
[Paragraph 2: Deeper explanation or nuance]
[Paragraph 3: Example or case study]
[Paragraph 4: Common misconceptions or caveats]
```

### Pattern: The Comparison Matrix
Use when the content compares multiple options.

```
[H2: "[Option A] vs [Option B]: Which is better for [use case]?"]
[Sentence 1: Direct recommendation with condition]
[Table: Feature-by-feature comparison with clear data]
[Paragraph: Analysis of key differences with recommendations by scenario]
[Callout: "Best for X: [Option]. Best for Y: [Option]."]
```

### Pattern: The How-To Guide
Use when the content teaches a process.

```
[H2: "How to [achieve outcome]"]
[Sentence 1: What you'll achieve and in how long]
[Numbered steps, each as its own H3]
  [Step name as H3]
  [What to do in 1-2 sentences]
  [Why it matters in 1 sentence]
  [Pro tip or common mistake in 1 sentence]
[Summary: Key takeaways in 2-3 sentences]
```

### Pattern: The Data-Driven Insight
Use when the content presents research findings or analysis.

```
[H2: The finding as a statement, not a question]
[Sentence 1: The key finding with specific numbers]
[Sentence 2: Source and methodology context]
[Paragraph 2: What this means in practical terms]
[Paragraph 3: How this compares to previous data or expectations]
[Table or chart: Supporting data visualization]
```

## Tone & Style Guidelines for Optimized Content

- **Be direct**: State things plainly. "X is Y" beats "It could be argued that X might be considered Y."
- **Be specific**: Numbers, names, dates, and examples beat generalizations.
- **Be structured**: Use the heading hierarchy as an outline. If you removed all body text and only left headings, would the structure make sense?
- **Be honest**: Acknowledge limitations, trade-offs, and uncertainty where appropriate. Credibility comes from nuance, not from claiming everything is amazing.
- **Be fresh**: Use current data, current examples, and current language. Avoid timeless-sounding prose that could have been written in any year.
- **Be human**: Optimized content should still sound like it was written by a knowledgeable person, not assembled by a machine. Let personality, opinion, and expertise come through.
